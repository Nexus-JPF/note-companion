## What Is an Obsidian Weekly Review?

**Short answer:** An Obsidian weekly review is a 30–45 minute session where you empty your inbox folder, rename, tag and file each note, add links, update your Maps of Content, and write a short plan for next week. Doing it weekly keeps the backlog small enough to clear in one sitting.

| | Daily inbox sweep | Weekly review | Monthly audit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Time | 5–10 min | 30–45 min | 1–2 hours |
| Goal | Get captures out of the inbox | File, tag, link and summarize the week's notes | Prune tags, archive stale notes, restructure MOCs |
| Best for | Heavy daily capture | Most Obsidian vaults | Large or long-lived vaults |

The weekly review comes from David Allen's [Getting Things Done](https://gettingthingsdone.com/what-is-gtd/). If you capture heavily every day, pair it with a [daily inbox routine](/blog/tame-obsidian-inbox-across-devices-ai-organization); freelancers can borrow the client-folder setup from our [Monday inbox ritual](/blog/freelance-monday-obsidian-inbox-ritual).

## Why Weekly Reviews Are a Game-Changer in Obsidian

A weekly review isn't just digital housekeeping—it's the heartbeat of a [second brain in Obsidian](/blog/how-to-automate-your-second-brain). Done right, it:

- Transforms loose notes into a structured, searchable knowledge base
- Prevents backlog paralysis by processing new info regularly
- Deepens understanding by connecting related ideas
- Helps you reflect and course-correct before small messes become big problems

Obsidian's folders, tags, wikilinks, and plugins make these reviews more powerful and less tedious than in traditional notebooks or word processors. By building this ritual, you create a vault that grows more useful over time.

## The Weekly Review Workflow: Step-By-Step

Here's how to tame your inbox and keep your vault humming—adapt these steps to fit your style and setup.

### 1. Schedule Your Review and Set Up a Template

Pick a recurring time: Sunday evenings, Friday afternoons, whatever works. Consistency is essential. Create a `templates/weekly-review.md` file with these sections:

```markdown
## Inbox triage
- [ ] Inbox emptied (notes processed: )
## Notes to link
## Ideas to expand
## Wins and pain points
## Focus for next week
```

Use Obsidian's [Templates plugin](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/templates) to insert this review note at the start of each session. This makes each review focused and repeatable.

### 2. Process the Inbox Folder

Dive into your `inbox/` folder and tackle each note. For every file:

- **Rename**: Give it a meaningful, action-oriented title (e.g. `research/2026-06-01-quantum-entanglement-summary.md` instead of `inbox/note1.md`).
- **Tag**: Apply clear, consistent tags like `#research`, `#meeting-note`, `#idea`. Avoid near-duplicates (`#meeting` vs. `#meetings`).
- **Move**: Relocate notes to fitting folders—`research/`, `projects/`, or `archive/`.

If your inbox is intimidatingly large, [Note Companion](https://www.notecompanion.ai)'s Organizer can suggest tags and folders for each note. Use them as a baseline, then adjust to your conventions. For a large one-off backlog, start with our [backlog triage guide](/blog/taming-backlog-strategies-process-unorganized-notes).

### 3. Link and Connect Your Notes

Wikilinks and backlinks are the magic that make Obsidian special. As you process each note:

- Add links to related notes (e.g., from a meeting note to `[[Project Apollo]]`).
- Use the [backlinks pane](https://obsidian.md/help/plugins/backlinks) to catch unlinked mentions—connect related ideas you might otherwise overlook.
- Ask Note Companion's vault chat questions like "Which of my notes mention entanglement?" to find related notes worth linking. See our guide on [connect ideas across your vault](/blog/using-ai-to-connect-ideas-across-your-vault).

### 4. Summarize and Distill Key Points

For dense notes—like research articles or meeting transcripts—add a brief summary at the top. Use a heading such as `## Summary` or `## Key Insights`. This makes scanning and future reviews quick and effective. Even a few sentences can save you from rereading pages later.

### 5. Update Your Maps of Content (MOCs)

If you use MOC notes as knowledge hubs (e.g., `research/moc-quantum-physics.md`), update them with new links, concepts, or resources added this week. This keeps your knowledge structure navigable and up to date. For habits that balance tags and MOCs, see [Maps of Content](/blog/tagging-vs-maps-of-content-small-habit-shifts-research-obsidian).

### 6. Reflect and Set Next Week's Focus

In your weekly review note, jot down:

- What organizational tweaks worked (or didn't)
- Where bottlenecks or confusion appeared
- What you want to improve or focus on next week—be specific (e.g., "Standardize all research tags" or "Link every project note to its MOC").

This reflection turns your review from a chore into a growth ritual.

## Real-World Scenario: Priya's Inbox Makeover

Before her review, Priya, a PhD student, had a vault that looked like this:

- **Inbox**: 25+ notes with generic names and inconsistent tags
- **Folders**: New notes dumped into `inbox/`, never sorted
- **Links**: Seldom used, so research and projects are siloed
- **Summaries**: Nonexistent, making it hard to recall what's important

After dedicating 30 minutes to her weekly review:

- Inbox is clear—notes are renamed (e.g., `research/entanglement-summary.md`), tagged, and properly filed
- Tags are standardized (`#research`, `#meeting-note`, `#idea`)
- Meeting notes link to their related project and MOC pages
- Summaries top her longer reading notes
- A review note in `journals/week-24-2026.md` logs her wins, pain points, and next steps

Priya now finds what she needs in seconds, not minutes. Her vault supports her learning, not her stress.

## Checklist: Your Weekly Obsidian Review

- [ ] Block dedicated time weekly for review
- [ ] Process and rename notes in the inbox folder
- [ ] Add consistent tags and move notes to correct folders
- [ ] Link notes with wikilinks and update backlinks
- [ ] Summarize long notes and highlight key points
- [ ] Update Map of Content notes
- [ ] Reflect on progress and plan next week's goals

## Tips for Making Your Weekly Review Stick

- Set a recurring calendar event or task-app reminder (Obsidian has no built-in reminders).
- Keep your review simple—just processing and linking is enough at first.
- Automate repetitive steps with plugins: Templater for inserting review checklists, QuickAdd for moving notes, or Note Companion for AI-powered suggestions.
- Don't get hung up on perfection—progress beats perfect organization.
- Celebrate your clean inbox with a quick win (maybe even a digital sticker in your daily note).
