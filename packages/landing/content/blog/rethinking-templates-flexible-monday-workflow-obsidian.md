# Rethinking Templates: A Flexible Monday Workflow for Obsidian

## Why Too Many Templates Backfire

Templates are one of Obsidian’s biggest strengths—but it’s easy to go overboard. When every note type has its own complex template, the result is friction: you spend more time filling out forms than capturing ideas or making connections. Instead of supporting your thinking, over-templating can box it in, making note-taking feel mechanical and uninspired. The trick is to use templates as subtle guides, not rigid frameworks.

This workflow will show you how to set up and use simple, open-ended templates for your Monday morning review, so you can organize, reflect, and plan—without sacrificing flexibility or creativity.

## Templates as Gentle Nudges, Not Detailed Scripts

Think of templates in Obsidian as prompts to spark reflection—not checklists to mindlessly complete. A good template should:

- Nudge you to process your ideas and tasks
- Leave space for free-form thought
- Be easy to adapt as your needs change

For example, replace lengthy meeting templates with a few key prompts:

- What decisions were made?
- What are the next steps?
- Any open questions?

This keeps your energy focused on substance rather than structure.

## Building a Simple, Adaptable Folder & Template Setup

To foster balance, organize your vault into a few intuitive folders:

- **inbox/** — For unprocessed notes: quick captures, meeting notes, research snippets
- **weekly/** — Weekly reviews and planning
- **projects/** — Working notes for ongoing initiatives
- **reference/** — Evergreen notes, literature, and resources

Create a minimalist template for your weekly review, e.g., `Templates/weekly-review.md`:

```markdown
# Weekly Review - {{date:YYYY-MM-DD}}

## Inbox Processing
- [ ] Process all notes in inbox/

## Highlights & Insights
- 

## Project Updates
- 

## Priorities for the Week
- 

```

This template prompts thoughtful review and planning, while leaving plenty of blank space for your own observations and connections.

## Monday Morning Workflow: Action Steps

Here’s a step-by-step routine to start your week in Obsidian:

1. **Open your Monday daily note** (e.g., `daily/2024-06-17.md`).
2. **Create a new weekly review note** in your `weekly/` folder using the `weekly-review` template.
3. **Process your inbox:**
   - Review each file in `inbox/`:
     - If it’s a fleeting idea, archive or delete.
     - If it’s actionable, move it into the appropriate `projects/` note or turn it into a task.
     - For reference material, tag as `#reference` and file in `reference/`.
4. **Capture insights:**
   - Use the Highlights & Insights section to jot down key learnings or connections made during the review.
   - Link to existing notes using [[wikilinks]].
5. **Update project notes:**
   - For each ongoing project, add any new tasks, notes, or updates in the relevant file (e.g., `projects/client-redesign.md`).
6. **Set your top 3 priorities for the week:**
   - Write a simple, focused list in the Priorities section.
7. **Schedule a reminder** for next Monday to repeat the routine.

By letting prompts guide you—rather than dictate your every move—you keep your system light, fast, and adaptable.

## Real-World Example: Lara, a Graduate Student

Lara manages coursework, research, and thesis writing in her Obsidian vault. She used to rely on detailed templates for every note, but it left her overwhelmed and slowed her down.

Now, her process is streamlined:

- Throughout the week, she drops lecture notes, ideas, and research clippings into `inbox/`.
- Monday morning, she creates `weekly/2024-06-17.md` from her template.
- She quickly sorts her inbox:
  - Literature notes tagged `#research` go into `projects/thesis-literature.md`.
  - Class summaries are moved into `reference/`.
  - Meeting notes get linked from relevant project files.
- In the Highlights & Insights section, she reflects on a recent breakthrough about her thesis topic, linking to `[[Concept_X]]`.
- Lara updates her thesis project note with next actions.
- She sets three concrete goals: finish her literature review, draft experiment methods, and prepare for her group presentation.

This workflow helps Lara stay organized and reflective, without wrestling with overly detailed templates.

## Where AI and Note Companion Save You Time

AI-powered plugins like Note Companion can make your Monday workflow smoother, without adding complexity:

- **Organizer Suggestions:** As you process your inbox, Note Companion proposes tags, folder destinations, or even improved note titles—helping you file notes quickly and consistently.
- **Transcription & Summarization:** If you capture YouTube lectures or podcasts, Note Companion can transcribe and summarize them directly into your inbox for easy review and linking.
- **Vault Chat:** When reviewing notes, you can @mention Note Companion in chat to surface related insights or connections across your vault, making your review more insightful.

Use AI to lighten the load, but always trust your own judgment to decide what structure (if any) a note really needs.

## Checklist: Preparing for Your Monday Review

- [ ] Create `inbox/` for raw notes and captures
- [ ] Set up a `weekly/` folder for reviews
- [ ] Save a minimal weekly review template with prompts
- [ ] Schedule a weekly Monday reminder
- [ ] Install Note Companion or similar for smart inbox processing
- [ ] Identify 2–3 project notes for regular updates
- [ ] Define tagging conventions for reference (#reference), research (#research), and tasks (#todo)

## Tips to Avoid Over-Templating

- Limit templates to 4–5 prompts
- Use open-ended questions, not exhaustive lists
- Let your notes evolve—add structure only if a pattern emerges over time
- Review templates every few months and remove unused sections
- Pair templates with flexible notes—don’t rely on templates for everything

## Wrapping Up

Templates should help you think better, not add friction. By starting each Monday with a lightweight, prompt-driven weekly review in Obsidian, you’ll shape your week with intention—while keeping your vault nimble and personal.

Balance structure and freedom, using tools like Note Companion to accelerate inbox processing or surface connections, but keep your templates light. This way, your vault remains a living, creative workspace—never a bureaucratic maze.
