# From Clips to Clarity: Structuring YouTube Notes in Obsidian for Notion and Roam Migrants

## Why YouTube Notes Need Structure in Obsidian

If you’re coming over from Notion or Roam, your video-based notes are probably full of links, highlights, and graph connections. YouTube is a goldmine for anyone learning online, but in Obsidian, you trade away databases and block refs for plain-text Markdown and powerful linking. Without a workflow, it’s easy for your vault to become a jumble of raw transcripts and scattered ideas.

This guide lays out a focused, actionable process for turning YouTube videos into fully integrated Obsidian notes. You’ll move from messy captures to structured, cross-linked insights—so your vault stays clean, searchable, and primed for growth.

## Step 1: Capture Videos with a Dedicated Inbox

Start by creating a folder named `inbox/youtube` in your vault. This is your holding area for raw video notes, mimicking the inbox idea from PARA or GTD—and it’s especially useful if you’re used to Notion’s database views or Roam’s daily pages.

Here’s how to use it:

- Whenever you find a video worth noting, create a new Markdown file in `inbox/youtube`.
- Use the video’s title as your note name, optionally with a date prefix for sorting (e.g., `2024-06-18 - The Psychology of Learning.md`).
- Paste the video link at the top of the note.
- Add initial tags like `#youtube` and `#video` for quick filtering.

This system keeps your main vault folders uncluttered and lets you batch-process video notes on your schedule.

## Step 2: Transcribe and Summarize with AI

Typing out video transcripts is a slog. Instead, let AI handle the busywork. Tools like Note Companion’s YouTube-to-notes feature generate transcripts directly into your note—right from Obsidian.

Here’s a practical workflow:

- Use Note Companion (or a similar plugin) to pull the transcript from your pasted YouTube link.
- Skim the transcript for major sections and break it up with timestamped headers (e.g., `## 3:15 Spaced Repetition Explained`).
- Add a summary section at the top capturing the video’s core ideas.
- Highlight actionable tips or quotes using bold or blockquotes for emphasis.

Example result:

```
# The Psychology of Learning

**Summary:** Explores how spaced repetition boosts long-term memory retention.

---

## 0:00 Introduction
[Transcript excerpt...]

## 3:15 Spaced Repetition Explained
[Transcript excerpt...]

## 8:40 Practical Tips
[Transcript excerpt...]
```

AI doesn’t just save time—it also helps you process more videos without falling behind. If you’re migrating from Notion or Roam, this will feel faster than any manual database import or block copying.

## Step 3: Organize Notes with Folders and Tags

Folder structure is crucial for long-term clarity. If you’re used to Notion’s filtered views or Roam’s graph, you’ll rely more on folders, tags, and links in Obsidian.

A PARA-inspired system works well:

- `inbox/youtube` — For raw, unprocessed video notes.
- `reference/youtube` — For cleaned-up, evergreen notes after processing.
- `project/learning` — For notes tied to active study, courses, or research.

After you’ve transcribed and summarized a note, move it from `inbox/youtube` to `reference/youtube` or the relevant project folder. Tag generously (e.g., `#psychology`, `#machinelearning`) and consider adding a `#processed` tag to finished notes.

Note Companion’s Organizer suggestions can help here too: it analyzes your note and proposes folders, tags, or improved titles—making organization faster and less error-prone, especially during a vault migration.

## Step 4: Build Connections with Wikilinks and Maps of Content

Obsidian’s real power comes from linking notes, not just storing them. Don’t let your YouTube notes sit in isolation:

- Use `[[wikilinks]]` to connect topics from the video to other relevant notes—like your `[[Literature Review]]` or `[[Active Projects]]`.
- Add backlinks from existing notes to your new video note, creating two-way connections.
- Maintain a Map of Content (MOC) for video resources (e.g., `mocs/video-learning.md`) where you list and link all your processed YouTube notes by topic or series.

This turns your vault into a navigable web, where insights from videos surface contextually alongside readings, research, or project work.

## Step 5: Review and Refine Your Video Notes

Migrating from Roam or Notion is a chance to build better habits. Unlike databases, Obsidian depends on your regular attention—so set a weekly or biweekly review to:

- Process new items in `inbox/youtube`.
- Polish summaries and highlight new insights after rewatching or reflection.
- Archive or delete outdated or irrelevant notes.
- Update your MOC for videos and ensure new links are added.

This review keeps your system lean and avoids the classic Obsidian “vault sprawl.”

## Real-World Scenario: Ana’s Migration from Roam

Ana, a graduate psychology student, is shifting her research workflow from Roam to Obsidian. She’s a heavy YouTube learner and wants her lectures to live alongside her research notes.

- She creates `inbox/youtube` and saves new video notes there, naming files like `2024-06-18 - Neuroplasticity.md`.
- She uses Note Companion to auto-transcribe each lecture, then breaks up the transcript by topic.
- Once processed, she moves notes to `reference/youtube` and tags them `#psychology #lecture`.
- She links concepts (e.g., `[[Memory Consolidation]]`) and adds each video to her `mocs/video-learning.md`.
- Every Sunday, Ana reviews her inbox, processes new videos, and updates links—keeping her vault organized and ready for exams or writing.

This approach saves Ana hours each week and ensures her video learning is always accessible and connected.

## Checklist: Your YouTube-to-Obsidian Workflow

- [ ] Create an `inbox/youtube` folder for raw video notes
- [ ] Save each video’s title, link, and date in a new note
- [ ] Use Note Companion (or similar) for AI transcription
- [ ] Break up transcripts into timestamped sections and write summaries
- [ ] Move processed notes to `reference/youtube` or relevant project folders
- [ ] Add topic tags and link to related notes with [[wikilinks]]
- [ ] Update a Map of Content for videos
- [ ] Schedule a recurring review to process and refine notes

## Final Thoughts

Moving your YouTube note-taking from Notion or Roam to Obsidian isn’t just a copy-paste job. It means embracing folders, Markdown, and explicit linking—but the payoff is a more durable, personal knowledge base.

With the right workflow—capture, transcribe, organize, link, and review—YouTube videos become a rich, discoverable layer in your vault, not a forgotten stack of transcripts. AI tools like Note Companion’s transcription and Organizer suggestions make the transition smoother, letting you focus on insights, not admin.

Start by building your inbox folder and capturing a few videos. As you process and connect your notes, you’ll find Obsidian’s plain-text approach more powerful than any database or graph view for long-term learning and retention.

Happy note-taking!
